## Three observation cards

### Supplier cybersecurity verification

**Status:** Company-disclosed potential risk; no specific incident established.

Siemens says distributed supply chains make cybersecurity control difficult and that many suppliers require substantial initial and recurring verification. It also identifies potential disruption and data exposure despite protective measures. [S1, printed pp. 26–27 / PDF pages 28–29](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:428ea18a-e7ab-4f93-a160-33908f1c3540/Siemens-Annual-Report-2025.pdf#page=28)

**Buyer follow-up:** Check the applicable supplier's cybersecurity evidence and component scope. This recommendation is an inference for procurement review, not a finding that Siemens or any supplier failed a control.

### Critical supply concentration

**Status:** Company-disclosed potential risk; no current shortage established.

Siemens identifies possible bottlenecks, delivery delays, quality problems, and cost increases from supply shocks and export restrictions, with additional exposure when critical components or services depend on a single source. [S1, “Supply chain management,” printed p. 27 / PDF page 29](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:428ea18a-e7ab-4f93-a160-33908f1c3540/Siemens-Annual-Report-2025.pdf#page=29)

**Buyer follow-up:** Request component-specific alternate-source and continuity evidence. The disclosure does not identify the actual sourcing arrangement for a buyer's part.

### Long-term project delivery exposure

**Status:** Company-disclosed potential risk; no contract failure established.

Siemens describes cost, execution, partner-performance and penalty exposure on long-term projects; fixed-price margins may differ from initial estimates. [S1, “Project-related risks,” printed p. 27 / PDF page 29](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:428ea18a-e7ab-4f93-a160-33908f1c3540/Siemens-Annual-Report-2025.pdf#page=29)

**Buyer follow-up:** Review capacity, schedule assumptions, and change-control terms for the relevant contract. This is a proposed review task, not an assessment of a specific project.

## One bounded follow-up branch for the UI

**Trigger:** The report's supplier cybersecurity verification disclosure.

**Question:** What public supplier assurance process addresses that exposure in the rail business?

**Budget:** One follow-up question; up to two official pages; stop after locating process evidence or record an evidence gap. This fixture's branch consulted S2 and S3.

**Result:** Siemens Mobility describes verification according to supplier criticality: declarations for low criticality, self-assessment for medium, and evidence for high. It also describes contractual requirements, checks of achieved capability, and supplier software bills of materials. This is evidence of the company's stated process, not proof that a particular supplier passed it. [S2](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/cybersecurity/certification-standards.html)

**Currency check:** Siemens' SCM page lists cybersecurity rules dated July 2026. The listing supports the existence of a newer rules document; this fixture does not claim to have audited its implementation or all clauses. [S3](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/company/about/supply-chain-management/scm-downloadcenter/)

**Outcome label:** “Assurance process found · supplier evidence still needed.” Do not mark the risk resolved or reduce a risk score automatically.
